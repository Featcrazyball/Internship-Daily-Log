Compared to previous weeks, this week was far more eventful. While the tasks 
themselves were simple, they were exhausting for an introvert like me. From 
Tuesday to Thursday, I was at the Marina Bay Sands Expo and Convention Centre 
for GovWare, marketing the web application developed by Momentum Z. This needed
a completely different skill set from what I have learnt in school. Every time 
I approached a stranger to explain our product, I had to work up the courage first. 
Monday and Friday were just as packed, with fixing user interface bugs, brainstorming 
ideas and preparing presentations.

Overall, this was probably the most tiring week of my internship, but also one of 
the most rewarding. Apart from the many freebies, GovWare at Singapore International 
Cyber Week exposed me to industry leaders and new technologies. It also showed me 
that my efforts at Nanyang Polytechnic were paying off.

At NYP, I put a lot of effort into going beyond what my modules required, which gave 
me a strong foundation in software development. Even though GovWare is a cybersecurity 
event, I could combine that foundation with my Cybersecurity specialisation (Y3S1) 
to spot patterns between how the solutions on display were built and how I had led my own 
project teams. This became especially clear when I spoke with an industry specialist from 
InsiderSecurity. He believed that solutions should be built around what the customer actually 
needs, not around what the developer thinks is a good idea. This matches NYP's recent 
change for Year 2 students, who now build web applications based on real company problem 
statements instead of the usual sustainability theme. Hearing the same idea from someone in the 
industry convinced me that it is the right way to build.

I also saw many interesting new solutions, and most of them used AI. Many booths used AI to 
support Cyber Threat Intelligence, and others used it for Endpoint Detection and Response. 
While neither idea is new, talking to the exhibitors gave me a much deeper understanding of 
how these solutions were designed and how they are actually deployed.

The moment that stayed with me the most happened on Wednesday. A man from the jewellery industry, 
with no background in IT, told me he wanted to build a super app for the whole of ASEAN, combining 
WeChat, WhatsApp, Shopee and Grab into one. He said the idea was for humanity, and that he felt the 
universe was calling him to do it. At first, I simply thought the world was full of eccentric people. 
Looking back, it taught me something more useful, having a big vision is very different from understanding 
what it takes to build it, or whether people actually need it. That connects directly to what I learnt 
from InsiderSecurity. A good solution starts from a real problem, not from one person's passion.
