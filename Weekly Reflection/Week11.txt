Compared to the previous weeks, this week was a lot more calm. 
On Monday, I went through the task list, prepared a document to 
set up the system design V4 I made a few weeks back, and made 
PGAi’s features configurable from the super-admin side.

For the rest of the week, I looked into ESET’s features and APIs, 
checked if a client’s FortiClient VPN was compliant with CTM Tier 5, 
planned how clients could run our hardening tool on their own, and 
attended an IMDA briefing on emerging cyber threats.

The most challenging task this week was planning the client hardening process. 
Having built the automated hardening tool, I was asked to design a way for clients 
to run it on their own endpoints and send the results back to us. However, the 
key requirement was that the script must be completely gone afterwards, so that 
clients cannot keep or reuse it.

Honestly, this was much harder than it sounds. Once a script is on someone else’s 
device, it is no longer fully in our control. Thus, I planned something like a handshake, 
where the hardening script is sent one piece at a time instead of all at once. Each 
piece only arrives after the previous one has run and reported back, so the full script 
never sits on the client’s device.

To put it in simple terms, it is like a teacher handing out an exam one question at a 
time and collecting each answer before giving the next. Even then, the student could still 
copy down a question. Ergo, I learnt that in security, the goal is often to make something 
difficult enough to not be worth it, rather than impossible.

Moreover, the FortiClient task taught me not to judge too quickly. A client asked if the 
free FortiClient VPN-Only was compliant with CTM Tier 5, so I made an Excel sheet to check 
it against every clause. This took longer than expected, as I was confused why they were 
using just a VPN for their entire security. However, I later learned that it was only for 
their employees’ devices brought from home.

Back in school, assignments came with all the information we needed. In the workplace, 
context is often missing, so asking questions early would have saved me time.

Apart from that, I would like to be honest about my week. Previously, I was constantly asking 
for work. However, from Tuesday onwards, I finished my tasks, reported them and waited for more 
work. In the meantime, I prepared for university admissions by writing READMEs for my other 
projects and planning for future projects.

Honestly, not asking for more work is something I picked up from some of the other interns. This 
was because I noticed a difference between us during our morning meetings. Most of the time, they 
have nothing to do. On the other hand, I am already planning in my head how to accomplish my tasks 
for the day. 

Earlier in my internship, I said that if one has no work, work will be assigned to them. This week 
showed me that it is not always the case, and that the quieter moments are up to me to use well.

Before I end off, I would like to say that a quiet week does not mean an empty one. The hardening plan
reminded me that security is about trade-offs, while the FortiClient check reminded me to ask before 
assuming. Not to mention, the free time gave me a chance to look back at my own projects and prepare 
for what comes after this internship. Moving forward, I hope to keep finding a good balance between
asking for work and investing in myself.
