This was my last week at Momentum Z. Most of it was spent drawing ER diagrams 
of PGAi's database so that future developers can understand the schema. Apart 
from that, I reviewed the Customer and Development SOPs, updated the developer
documentation, made the hardening tool update Windows, and helped analyse the 
firewall logs of an organisation that had a data leak.

The ER diagrams took almost three days. This was because the database has 125
tables and no prior documentation, so I had to work out every relationship myself.
Honestly, my Database Management Systems module really helped here. Drawing ER
diagrams was basically one of my assignments, just on a much larger scale. 
Without it, I would not have known where to start.

Compared to the previous weeks, this week was about leaving things behind rather 
than building new ones. Funnily enough, in Week 1, I was told that I was "not paid 
enough" to document so much. Yet in my final week, documentation was the main task. 
The difference is that this time, it was for someone else to use.

Looking back, when I first started, I thought the workplace would be like school, 
where every step must be documented carefully. However, I soon learnt that what
mattered more was the result. I also thought I would only be doing cybersecurity. 
Instead, I ended up developing PGAi, learning Go and C#, conducting a network VA, 
and even marketing at GovWare.

Along the way, I met many people who made this internship what it was. Shane Chiang,
our CEO, was very lax, but strict when needed. The other interns made the place lively,
or else it would have just been boring work. Not to mention, my IS, Mr Yew Mun, guided 
me throughout my internship journey, always explaining why things are done the way they are.

Of course, there were many difficulties. When I did not understand something, such as
the Python script in Week 1, I asked the people around me. When I lacked a skill, such
as Go or system design, I self-studied it. When I had never used a MacBook, I simply
found one and figured it out. Thus, I learnt that most difficulties can be overcome by 
asking, learning or just trying.

In addition, I learnt a lot about working in society. I now understand the different
cybersecurity certifications, such as CSA's Cyber Essentials and Cyber Trust Mark, and 
why organisations need a DPO under the PDPA. I also saw how an organisation operates, 
where everyone, no matter their role, helps towards the same goal. Moreover, I saw how 
legal constraints make work harder. Emails were left out when web scraping due to data 
privacy laws, and today, I had to remove all organisation data from my laptop during offboarding.

Before I end off, I would like to thank everyone at Momentum Z. Coming from Montfort, I 
have always stuck by the motto "Age Quod Agis", meaning do well in whatever you do. Over
these 12 weeks, I hope I have done just that.
